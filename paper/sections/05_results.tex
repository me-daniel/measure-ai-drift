\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

All results are reported at the second rescripting turn (slice~2), aggregated across six vignettes. Statistics are computed over 330 experiment runs (11 models $\times$ 6 vignettes $\times$ 5 temperatures, 20 trials each). Tests are exploratory. Kruskal--Wallis $H$ is reported with $\eta^2$ effect sizes, and pairwise contrasts use Mann--Whitney $U$ with Bonferroni correction over $\binom{11}{2}=55$ comparisons.

\subsection{Plan Validity and Strategy Distribution}
\label{sec:validity}

Before analysing stability, the plan extraction step must be validated. The structured format turns out to be essentially free: overall validity is 99.9\%, with every model at or above 99.3\% (Figure~\ref{fig:validity}), and validity does not vary meaningfully with temperature. The fused prompt reliably elicits structured plan output, and the stability metrics below are computed over near-complete data. The six malformed trials are treated as missing data (Section~\ref{sec:metrics}).

Across 13{,}181 declared strategy picks, all six taxonomy categories are used, though not uniformly: \texttt{sensory\_modulation} (27.0\%), \texttt{cognitive\_reframe} (25.7\%), and \texttt{self\_empowerment} (19.8\%) dominate, while \texttt{safety} (10.6\%), \texttt{social\_support} (9.9\%), and \texttt{confrontation} (6.9\%) form a long tail (Figure~\ref{fig:strategies}). No category exceeds a third of picks, in contrast to the 87.7\% single-category skew of the initial taxonomy (Appendix~\ref{app:taxonomy}).

These distributional differences are a prerequisite for interpreting the consistency results that follow. A model that always selects the same two strategies will score high on Jaccard regardless of temperature, while a model with a wider repertoire has more room to vary. OLMo~3.1~32B and Qwen~3.5~122B concentrate most (73\% and 71\% of picks in their top two categories), the proprietary models follow (65--67\%), and Mistral Large~3 and Mistral Small~4 spread widest (46--49\%).

\subsection{Plan Consistency (Method 1)}
\label{sec:results-jaccard}

Figure~\ref{fig:jaccard} presents the headline result: decision-level stability is high but far from universal. Median pairwise Jaccard per run ranges from 1.000 (Mistral Medium~3.5, Mistral Small~3.2, OLMo~3.1~32B, Qwen~3.5~122B) down to 0.689--0.727 (Qwen~3.5~397B, Llama~3.3~70B, Qwen~3.5~27B). Even at fixed temperature and identical context, the least stable models change their declared therapeutic strategy in roughly one of three trial pairs.

\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \makebox[\textwidth]{\includegraphics[width=1.12\textwidth]{fig_5_2_jaccard}}
  \caption{Median pairwise Jaccard by model and temperature, grouped by family. Shaded bands show IQR. The dashed line marks perfect consistency, the dotted line the random baseline ($J \approx 0.2$). Higher is more consistent.}
  \label{fig:jaccard}
\end{figure}

Temperature degrades decision consistency overall (pooled Spearman $\rho=-0.210$, $p<.001$; Kruskal--Wallis $H(4)=19.2$, $p<.001$, $\eta^2=0.058$): the grand median falls from 0.933 at $T{=}0.0$ to 0.730 at $T{=}0.6$. But the effect is far from uniform. Per-model correlations separate three groups: OLMo~3.1~32B ($\rho=-0.65$), Qwen~3.5~122B ($\rho=-0.56$), and Qwen~3.5~27B ($\rho=-0.49$) degrade significantly, the entire Mistral family shows no significant trend, and the proprietary ceilings are flat ($\rho \approx +0.03$ for both GPT-5.4 and Sonnet~4.6). The most capable models hold their therapeutic decisions stable across the whole sampled range.

Greedy decoding, however, is not always optimal. Eight of the eleven models reach their best mean Jaccard at a non-zero temperature. The pattern is clearest in the Mistral family: Mistral Small~4 rises from 0.777 at $T{=}0.0$ to 0.881 at $T{=}0.15$, Mistral Large~3 from 0.796 to 0.848 at $T{=}0.15$, and Llama~3.3~70B from 0.719 to 0.775 at $T{=}0.075$. The post-hoc addition follows the same pattern: Mistral Medium~3.5 peaks at a perfect 1.000 mean at $T{=}0.075$, above its 0.979 at $T{=}0.0$. Whether these differences reflect a genuine benefit of low sampling variance or sample variation ($n=6$ vignettes per cell) cannot be determined from these results alone, but they are consistent with Mistral's vendor default of $T{=}0.15$.

Model differences are substantial (Kruskal--Wallis $H(10)=79.1$, $p<.001$, $\eta^2=0.240$; 13 of 55 Bonferroni-corrected pairs significant). Modal-set agreement, the fraction of trials whose declared set equals the run's most common set, tells the same story (Figure~\ref{fig:modal}), from 0.972 (Mistral Medium~3.5) down to 0.670 (Qwen~3.5~27B).

\subsection{Response Similarity (Method 2)}
\label{sec:results-bertscore}

BERTScore F1 measures semantic similarity between the response texts, independent of the plan block. The pooled temperature effect is roughly twice as strong as for Jaccard ($\rho=-0.461$, $p<.001$; $H(4)=78.1$, $p<.001$, $\eta^2=0.237$), with the median falling from 0.787 at $T{=}0.0$ to 0.705 at $T{=}0.6$ (Figure~\ref{fig:bertscore}). Rising temperature changes response wording before it changes strategy selection: models say the same thing differently before they decide differently.

BERTScore is, however, insensitive to strategy changes. A model that switches from \emph{confrontation} to \emph{cognitive reframe} produces surface text similar enough that BERTScore barely registers the difference, since different rescripting strategies share the same conversational framing, sentence length, and register. BERTScore therefore serves as a check on response style rather than as a measure of decision stability. Between-model differences are again large ($H(10)=92.1$, $p<.001$, $\eta^2=0.280$; 21 of 55 pairs significant), with Mistral Medium~3.5 highest (mean F1 0.816) and Mistral Small~4 lowest (0.697).

\subsection{Vignette Effects}
\label{sec:results-vignettes}

Patient presentation matters little relative to model and temperature. Vignette differences are not significant for decision consistency ($H(5)=8.2$, $p=.145$, $\eta^2=0.025$) and only marginally so for response similarity ($H(5)=12.2$, $p=.033$, $\eta^2=0.037$): patient profile affects response wording more than strategy selection. Median Jaccard spans a narrow 0.821 (avoidant, cooperative, resistant) to 1.000 (skeptic). Stability is predominantly a \emph{model} property, not a \emph{patient-difficulty} property, though individual model--vignette combinations can still deviate from the aggregate, which keeps per-vignette reporting useful for spotting where a specific model breaks down. The full per-vignette heatmaps appear in Appendix~\ref{app:vignettes}.

\subsection{Cross-Metric Relations}
\label{sec:results-correlations}

The three methods are related but not redundant. Decision- and response-level consistency correlate moderately (Spearman $\rho=0.548$, $p<.001$, $n=330$): models with more consistent strategy selection also tend to produce more similar text, but not strongly enough for one metric to substitute for the other. Alignment correlates only weakly with Jaccard ($\rho=0.213$) and moderately with BERTScore ($\rho=0.353$), consistent with its role as a near-ceiling validation check rather than a third stability axis.

Figure~\ref{fig:correlations} shows how the models arrange on the two stability axes. Mistral Medium~3.5 anchors the upper right, four models sit at perfect median decision consistency with clearly different response-level scores, and Qwen~3.5~27B occupies the lower left. Mistral Small~4 presents an interesting case: moderate decision consistency but the lowest response similarity in the set, so even when it selects the same strategies, its wording varies considerably.

\subsection{Plan--Response Alignment (Method 3)}
\label{sec:results-alignment}

Models largely do what they declare. Mean alignment ranges from 1.000 (Sonnet~4.6) and 0.998 (GPT-5.4) down to 0.810 (Mistral Small~4), and it is temperature-independent ($\rho=-0.066$, $p=.23$). Models do not get worse at implementing their declared strategies as temperature rises: they select \emph{different} strategies more often (lower Jaccard) but implement whichever strategy they select equally well (Figure~\ref{fig:alignment}).

Model differences on alignment are nonetheless the largest of the three metrics ($H(10)=139.3$, $p<.001$, $\eta^2=0.423$; 26 of 55 pairs significant), driven by the gap between the proprietary ceilings ($\geq 0.998$) and the weaker open models. The near-ceiling scores validate Method~1: declared plans are implemented, so Jaccard measures genuine decision variability rather than label noise. Judge accounting: of 6{,}600 trials, none failed with judge errors, 6 were unjudgeable (invalid plans, excluded as missing data), and 9 of the 6{,}000 March trials (0.15\%) were re-judged by the Gemini~3.1 Pro fallback, none of the 600 July trials.

\subsection{Robustness to Model-Set Enlargement: Mistral Medium 3.5}
\label{sec:robustness-results}

The post-hoc addition lands at the top of the stability ranking. Mistral Medium~3.5 attains a perfect median Jaccard of 1.000, the highest mean BERTScore F1 in the set (0.816), the highest modal-set agreement (0.972), perfect plan validity, and mean alignment of 0.961 across its 30 conditions. Its Jaccard profile stays at or near ceiling across all five temperatures. Its consistency does not come from a narrow repertoire either: its pooled strategy distribution is broader than that of the proprietary models (59\% top-two share), so its selections vary across patients but not across runs of the same patient.

Enlarging the pairwise family from $\binom{10}{2}=45$ to $\binom{11}{2}=55$ comparisons tightens the Bonferroni correction. Exactly two marginal pairs among the original ten models lost significance (Jaccard: Llama~3.3~70B vs.\ Qwen~3.5~122B, $p_{\text{bonf}}$ $0.046 \to 0.056$; alignment: GPT-5.4 vs.\ Mistral Large~3, $0.045 \to 0.055$). No pairwise conclusion reversed direction, no pair became newly significant among the original ten, and the BERTScore pairwise structure is unchanged. All substantive findings of the original study hold under model-set enlargement.
