\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

All results are reported at the second rescripting turn (slice~2), aggregated across six vignettes. Statistics are computed over 390 experiment runs (13 models $\times$ 6 vignettes $\times$ 5 temperatures, 20 trials each). Tests are exploratory. Kruskal--Wallis $H$ is reported with $\eta^2$ effect sizes, and pairwise contrasts use Mann--Whitney $U$ with Bonferroni correction over $\binom{13}{2}=78$ comparisons.

\subsection{Plan Validity and Strategy Distribution}
\label{sec:validity}

Before analysing stability, the plan extraction step must be validated. The structured format turns out to be essentially free: overall validity is 99.8\%, with every model at or above 98.3\% (Command~A lowest; Figure~\ref{fig:validity}), and validity does not vary meaningfully with temperature. The fused prompt reliably elicits structured plan output, and the stability metrics below are computed over near-complete data. The seventeen malformed trials are treated as missing data (Section~\ref{sec:metrics}).

Across 15{,}561 declared strategy picks, all six taxonomy categories are used, though not uniformly: \texttt{sensory\_modulation} (26.7\%), \texttt{cognitive\_reframe} (24.5\%), and \texttt{self\_empowerment} (20.2\%) dominate, while \texttt{social\_support} (11.1\%), \texttt{safety} (9.6\%), and \texttt{confrontation} (8.0\%) form a long tail (Figure~\ref{fig:strategies}). No category exceeds a third of picks, in contrast to the 87.7\% single-category skew of the initial taxonomy (Appendix~\ref{app:taxonomy}).

These distributional differences are a prerequisite for interpreting the consistency results that follow. A model that always selects the same two strategies will score high on Jaccard regardless of temperature, while a model with a wider repertoire has more room to vary. OLMo~3.1~32B and Qwen~3.5~122B concentrate most (73\% and 71\% of picks in their top two categories), the proprietary models follow (65--67\%), and Command~A, Mistral Large~3, and Mistral Small~4 spread widest (46--49\%).

\subsection{Plan Consistency (Method 1)}
\label{sec:results-jaccard}

Figure~\ref{fig:jaccard} presents the headline result: decision-level stability is high but far from universal. Median pairwise Jaccard per run ranges from 1.000 (GPT-5.4, Mistral Medium~3.5, Mistral Small~3.2, OLMo~3.1~32B, Qwen~3.5~122B) down to 0.689--0.727 (Qwen~3.5~397B, Llama~3.3~70B, Mistral Large~2, Qwen~3.5~27B). Even at fixed temperature and identical context, the least stable models change their declared therapeutic strategy in roughly one of three trial pairs.

\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \makebox[\textwidth]{\includegraphics[width=1.12\textwidth]{fig_5_2_jaccard}}
  \caption{Median pairwise Jaccard by model and temperature, grouped by family. Shaded bands show IQR. The dashed line marks perfect consistency, the dotted line the random baseline ($J \approx 0.2$). Higher is more consistent.}
  \label{fig:jaccard}
\end{figure}

Temperature degrades decision consistency overall (pooled Spearman $\rho=-0.209$, $p<.001$; Kruskal--Wallis $H(4)=22.6$, $p<.001$, $\eta^2=0.058$): the grand median falls from 0.933 at $T{=}0.0$ to 0.730 at $T{=}0.6$. But the effect is far from uniform. Per-model correlations separate three groups: OLMo~3.1~32B ($\rho=-0.65$), Qwen~3.5~122B ($\rho=-0.56$), and Qwen~3.5~27B ($\rho=-0.49$) degrade significantly, the Mistral family and Command~A show no significant trend, and the proprietary ceilings are flat ($\rho=+0.03$ for Sonnet~4.6, $+0.09$ for GPT-5.4). The most capable models hold their therapeutic decisions stable across the whole sampled range.

Greedy decoding, however, is not always optimal. Eleven of the thirteen models reach their best mean Jaccard at a non-zero temperature. The pattern is clearest in the Mistral family: Mistral Small~4 rises from 0.777 at $T{=}0.0$ to 0.881 at $T{=}0.15$, Mistral Large~3 from 0.796 to 0.848 at $T{=}0.15$, and Llama~3.3~70B from 0.719 to 0.775 at $T{=}0.075$. The post-hoc additions follow the same pattern: Mistral Medium~3.5 peaks at a perfect 1.000 mean at $T{=}0.075$, above its 0.979 at $T{=}0.0$, Command~A rises from 0.832 to 0.861 at $T{=}0.15$, and Mistral Large~2 from 0.721 to 0.806 at $T{=}0.075$. Whether these differences reflect a genuine benefit of low sampling variance or sample variation ($n=6$ vignettes per cell) cannot be determined from these results alone, but they are consistent with Mistral's vendor default of $T{=}0.15$.

Model differences are substantial (Kruskal--Wallis $H(12)=117.5$, $p<.001$, $\eta^2=0.302$; 25 of 78 Bonferroni-corrected pairs significant). Modal-set agreement, the fraction of trials whose declared set equals the run's most common set, tells the same story (Figure~\ref{fig:modal}), from 0.993 (GPT-5.4) and 0.972 (Mistral Medium~3.5) down to 0.670 (Qwen~3.5~27B).

\subsection{Response Similarity (Method 2)}
\label{sec:results-bertscore}

BERTScore F1 measures semantic similarity between the response texts, independent of the plan block. The pooled temperature effect is roughly twice as strong as for Jaccard ($\rho=-0.474$, $p<.001$; $H(4)=96.3$, $p<.001$, $\eta^2=0.248$), with the median falling from 0.784 at $T{=}0.0$ to 0.705 at $T{=}0.6$ (Figure~\ref{fig:bertscore}). Rising temperature changes response wording before it changes strategy selection: models say the same thing differently before they decide differently.

BERTScore is, however, insensitive to strategy changes. A model that switches from \emph{confrontation} to \emph{cognitive reframe} produces surface text similar enough that BERTScore barely registers the difference, since different rescripting strategies share the same conversational framing, sentence length, and register. BERTScore therefore serves as a check on response style rather than as a measure of decision stability. Between-model differences are again large ($H(12)=95.2$, $p<.001$, $\eta^2=0.245$; 22 of 78 pairs significant), with Mistral Medium~3.5 highest (mean F1 0.816) and Mistral Small~4 lowest (0.697).

\subsection{Vignette Effects}
\label{sec:results-vignettes}

Patient presentation matters little relative to model and temperature. Vignette differences are not significant for decision consistency ($H(5)=5.3$, $p=.384$, $\eta^2=0.014$) but reach significance for response similarity ($H(5)=16.3$, $p=.006$, $\eta^2=0.042$): patient profile affects response wording, not which strategies are selected, and the wording effect is small next to the model effect ($\eta^2$ 0.042 vs.\ 0.245). Median Jaccard spans a narrow 0.775 (resistant) to 1.000 (skeptic). Stability is predominantly a \emph{model} property, not a \emph{patient-difficulty} property, though individual model--vignette combinations can still deviate from the aggregate, which keeps per-vignette reporting useful for spotting where a specific model breaks down. The full per-vignette heatmaps appear in Appendix~\ref{app:vignettes}.

\subsection{Cross-Metric Relations}
\label{sec:results-correlations}

The three methods are related but not redundant. Decision- and response-level consistency correlate moderately (Spearman $\rho=0.568$, $p<.001$, $n=390$): models with more consistent strategy selection also tend to produce more similar text, but not strongly enough for one metric to substitute for the other. Alignment correlates only weakly with Jaccard ($\rho=0.264$) and moderately with BERTScore ($\rho=0.385$), consistent with its role as a near-ceiling validation check rather than a third stability axis.

Figure~\ref{fig:correlations} shows how the models arrange on the two stability axes. Mistral Medium~3.5 anchors the upper right, five models sit at perfect median decision consistency with clearly different response-level scores, and Qwen~3.5~27B occupies the lower left. Mistral Small~4 presents an interesting case: moderate decision consistency but the lowest response similarity in the set, so even when it selects the same strategies, its wording varies considerably.

\subsection{Plan--Response Alignment (Method 3)}
\label{sec:results-alignment}

Models largely do what they declare. Mean alignment ranges from 1.000 (GPT-5.4 and Sonnet~4.6) down to 0.810 (Mistral Small~4), and it is temperature-independent ($\rho=-0.058$, $p=.25$). Models do not get worse at implementing their declared strategies as temperature rises: they select \emph{different} strategies more often (lower Jaccard) but implement whichever strategy they select equally well (Figure~\ref{fig:alignment}).

Model differences on alignment are nonetheless the largest of the three metrics ($H(12)=160.7$, $p<.001$, $\eta^2=0.413$; 39 of 78 pairs significant), driven by the gap between the proprietary ceilings (1.000) and the weaker open models. The near-ceiling scores validate Method~1: declared plans are implemented, so Jaccard measures genuine decision variability rather than label noise. Judge accounting: of 7{,}800 trials, none failed with judge errors, 17 were unjudgeable (invalid plans, 10 of them Command~A's, excluded as missing data), and 9 trials (0.12\%) were re-judged by the Gemini~3.1 Pro fallback.

\subsection{Robustness to Model-Set Changes}
\label{sec:robustness-results}

The first post-hoc addition lands at the top of the stability ranking. Mistral Medium~3.5 attains a perfect median Jaccard of 1.000, the highest mean BERTScore F1 in the set (0.816), the second-highest modal-set agreement (0.972), perfect plan validity, and mean alignment of 0.961 across its 30 conditions. Its Jaccard profile stays at or near ceiling across all five temperatures. This ceiling is not the sampling artefact diagnosed for GPT-5.4 below: Medium~3.5's BERTScore falls by 0.13 across the sweep ($\rho=-0.66$), the steepest response-level temperature reaction among the top-ranked models, so temperature demonstrably reaches the model and it is the decisions that hold still while the wording drifts. Its consistency does not come from a narrow repertoire either: its pooled strategy distribution is broader than that of the proprietary models (59\% top-two share), so its selections vary across patients but not across runs of the same patient.

The second addition behaves like an ordinary mid-field model, which is itself informative: Command~A combines one of the broadest strategy repertoires in the set (47\% top-two share) with mid-ranking decision consistency (median Jaccard 0.812), a genuine temperature response, and the framework's standard low-temperature optimum at $T{=}0.15$. The stricter collection route (pinned provider, enforced parameter forwarding) produces no qualitatively different behaviour from the original cohort.

The third addition sharpens the architecture question into a generation question. Mistral Large~2, the 123B dense Mistral flagship of 2024, lands in the bottom tier: median Jaccard 0.712, the second-lowest modal-set agreement (0.717), and mean BERTScore 0.736, despite perfect plan validity and high alignment (0.974). It implements what it declares, it just declares differently across runs. The 111--128B dense class thus spans the entire ranking, from Large~2 near the bottom through Command~A in the mid-field to Medium~3.5 at the ceiling, and the two Mistral models at either end differ mainly in training generation. Their Jaccard gap is Bonferroni-significant, the sharpest within-family contrast in the set.

The GPT-5.4 re-collection turns a measurement artefact into a finding. With temperature verifiably applied, GPT-5.4's mean decision consistency stays between 0.970 and 1.000 at every sampled temperature while its BERTScore declines from 0.770 to 0.745, the same decisions-stable-wording-drifts signature as Sonnet~4.6. The flat profile that the original collection produced for the wrong reason (the parameter was never applied) turns out to be genuine: GPT-5.4 is the most decision-stable model in the set (modal-set agreement 0.993), and its stability survives real sampling variance rather than reflecting its absence. At the trial level the two proprietary ceilings differ in kind. GPT-5.4 produces a single strategy set across all 20 trials in 28 of its 30 conditions, whereas Sonnet~4.6 produces two or three distinct sets in roughly half of its conditions at every temperature, including $T{=}0.0$. The two models are equally aligned (1.000) but differently deterministic, and Sonnet's non-monotonic median profile in Figure~\ref{fig:jaccard} reflects this temperature-independent baseline stochasticity passed through a median over six vignettes, not a temperature effect ($\rho=+0.03$, $p=.88$).

Each enlargement tightens the Bonferroni correction, from $\binom{10}{2}=45$ comparisons in the original design through 55 and 66 to now $\binom{13}{2}=78$. The earlier enlargements moved a handful of marginal pairs just below the significance boundary; the present one moves none. Among the previous twelve models no pairwise conclusion changes status on any metric: the closest calls sit just outside the boundary (Jaccard: GPT-5.4 vs.\ Qwen~3.5~122B, $p_{\text{bonf}}=0.06$; BERTScore: Mistral Small~3.2 vs.\ Sonnet~4.6, $p_{\text{bonf}}=0.06$) or just inside it (alignment: Command~A vs.\ Mistral Small~4, $p_{\text{bonf}}=0.049$), no conclusion reversed direction, and none became newly significant. All substantive findings of the original study hold under model-set enlargement and under the GPT-5.4 correction. The one change of statistical status concerns vignettes, not models: the response-similarity vignette effect, at the boundary in the original set ($p=.052$), is significant with thirteen models (Section~\ref{sec:results-vignettes}), while the decision-level vignette effect stays non-significant.
